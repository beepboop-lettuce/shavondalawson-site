The Cost of Being the Woman Who Builds Everything

By Shavonda Lawson

Everybody loves a woman who builds something from nothing.

They love the announcement.

The grand opening.

The beautiful pictures.

The title of CEO.

They love hearing how she turned a dream into a business.

But very few people talk about what building that dream actually costs her.

And I’m not just talking about money.

I’m talking about the nights you lie awake wondering how you’re going to make everything work.

The moments you have to smile for everyone else when you’re carrying more than anyone realizes.

The sacrifices your children sometimes see before they are old enough to understand them.

The birthdays, the dinners, the quiet moments, and the personal peace that entrepreneurship can demand.

I know those moments because I’ve lived them.

When I became a childcare entrepreneur, I wasn’t simply opening a business. I was creating a place where other parents could feel confident leaving the people they love most.

That’s an enormous responsibility.

Parents trust us with their babies, their toddlers, their growing children, and their hopes for the future.

Teachers depend on us for leadership, stability, and opportunities to grow.

Families count on us to be consistent.

And somewhere in the middle of all those responsibilities, there’s me.

Shavonda.

Not the CEO.

Not the founder.

Just the woman.

The woman who sometimes needs someone to ask how she’s doing without needing something in return.

I think women entrepreneurs, especially mothers, become so accustomed to being strong that we forget we’re allowed to need support.

We become the problem-solvers.

The decision-makers.

The people everyone calls when something goes wrong.

And eventually, we can begin to believe that being needed is the same thing as being fulfilled.

It’s not.

One of the greatest lessons entrepreneurship has taught me is that building a successful business should not require losing yourself in the process.

I’m still learning how to live that lesson.

I’ve had seasons when my business received the best parts of me and my personal life received whatever energy I had left.

I’ve had moments when I questioned whether the sacrifices were worth it.

And I’ve had to remind myself that the little girl who once dreamed of a meaningful life deserves to enjoy the life she’s creating.

Not just survive it.

When I look at my children, I want them to see more than a mother who worked hard.

I want them to see a woman who pursued her dreams without giving up on herself.

I want them to understand that success isn’t simply measured by how many businesses you own, how many people you employ, or how impressive your title sounds.

Sometimes success looks like setting a boundary.

Sometimes it looks like asking for help.

Sometimes it looks like admitting you’re tired.

And sometimes it looks like deciding that your peace matters just as much as your purpose.

I’m no longer interested in building a life that looks successful from the outside but leaves me empty on the inside.

I want both.

I want meaningful work and meaningful relationships.

I want thriving businesses and peaceful evenings.

I want to create opportunities for other people while remembering that I deserve opportunities to grow, rest, and experience joy, too.

And I want other women building businesses to hear this:

You do not have to prove your strength by carrying everything alone.

You do not have to earn rest by reaching exhaustion.

You do not have to apologize for wanting more than survival.

The dream matters.

But so does the dreamer.

I am incredibly proud of what I’ve built.

I’m also learning to be proud of the woman I’m becoming outside of what I’ve built.

Because at the end of the day, I don’t want my greatest accomplishment to be that I created something everyone admired.

I want my greatest accomplishment to be that I created a life I was actually present enough to enjoy.

And that is the kind of success I’m working toward now.

With love and intention,

Shavonda Lawson
