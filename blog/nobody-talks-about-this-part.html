Nobody Talks About This Part of Being a CEO

By Shavonda Lawson | Founder & CEO, The Nest Montessori

Let’s talk about something that doesn’t always make it onto social media.

The uncomfortable side of entrepreneurship.

Not the ribbon cuttings.

Not the professional photos.

Not the exciting announcements about new opportunities.

I’m talking about the decisions you have to make when nobody is standing beside you telling you what to do.

The conversations you don’t want to have.

The responsibilities that don’t disappear just because you’re tired.

And the reality that sometimes the person everyone looks to for answers is still trying to figure things out herself.

Welcome to leadership.

Being the Boss Doesn’t Mean Being Free

I think one of the biggest misconceptions about entrepreneurship is that owning a business automatically gives you freedom.

And yes, entrepreneurship can create incredible opportunities.

But there’s another side.

When you own a business, the responsibility doesn’t end when you leave the building.

You’re thinking about your employees, your customers, your finances, your reputation, and what needs to happen tomorrow.

You can delegate tasks, but you cannot completely delegate accountability.

That’s something I’ve learned firsthand.

I’ve also learned that being busy doesn’t necessarily mean you’re being productive.

As a CEO, my job isn’t to personally handle every situation.

My job is to build an organization that can function effectively without requiring me to make every decision.

And believe me, that takes practice.

Leadership Will Challenge Your Personality

I’m naturally passionate about the things I believe in.

When I have a vision, I want to see it come to life.

But leadership has taught me that passion alone isn’t enough.

You need patience.

You need boundaries.

You need clear expectations.

And sometimes you need to have conversations that make everyone in the room uncomfortable, including yourself.

I’ve had to learn that being a good leader doesn’t mean making everyone happy.

It means being fair, consistent, and willing to make decisions that support the long-term health of the organization.

I’ve also learned that you can care deeply about people and still hold them accountable.

Those two things are not opposites.

In fact, I believe accountability is one of the ways we demonstrate respect for the people and organizations we’re responsible for.

The Importance of Building the Right Team

One of the most valuable lessons I’ve learned is that you cannot build something meaningful by yourself.

You need people who understand the vision.

People who are willing to take ownership.

People who can respectfully challenge your thinking and help move the organization forward.

But building a strong team also requires you to become a stronger leader.

You have to communicate clearly, define responsibilities, and create systems that support accountability.

You cannot expect people to read your mind.

And you cannot hold someone accountable for expectations you’ve never communicated.

That’s leadership 101, but it’s a lesson worth repeating.

Sometimes Growth Looks Like Letting Go

Growth isn’t always about opening another location, increasing revenue, or adding more people to your team.

Sometimes growth looks like changing your mind.

Sometimes it’s acknowledging that a strategy isn’t working.

Sometimes it’s stepping away from something you’ve invested significant time and energy into.

And sometimes it’s recognizing that the version of you who started the business cannot be the same version of you who leads it into the future.

That last one has been especially meaningful to me.

As entrepreneurs, we spend so much time trying to grow our businesses that we sometimes forget we have to grow ourselves too.

Faith, Perspective, and the Bigger Picture

My faith is an important part of how I navigate entrepreneurship.

It doesn’t mean I never experience uncertainty.

It doesn’t mean every decision is easy.

And it certainly doesn’t mean that every plan unfolds exactly how I imagined.

But faith reminds me that my circumstances do not have to determine my perspective.

I can be grateful for what I’ve accomplished while still acknowledging the work ahead.

I can celebrate progress without pretending everything is perfect.

And I can believe in the vision while remaining flexible about the path.

A Reminder to the Entrepreneur Reading This

If you’re building something right now, I want you to remember a few things.

You don’t have to know everything.

You do need to remain willing to learn.

You don’t have to do everything yourself.

You do need to build systems and relationships that support your vision.

You don’t have to get every decision right.

You do need to take responsibility for the decisions you make.

And please, don’t become so consumed with building a successful business that you forget to build a fulfilling life.

I’m learning that lesson too.

This CEO journey is still teaching me, stretching me, and challenging me.

But I wouldn’t trade the growth for anything.

Until the next chapter,
