When the World Gets Loud, Let Your Work Speak

By Shavonda Lawson

There is a particular kind of heartbreak that comes from watching something you’ve poured your life into become the subject of conversations you cannot control.

It’s difficult to explain unless you’ve experienced it.

You spend years building a reputation.

Years earning trust.

Years working to create something that matters.

And then you discover how quickly people can form opinions based on a small piece of a much larger story.

As a founder and CEO, I’ve learned that building a business in the public eye requires more than passion and determination.

It requires resilience.

It requires humility.

And sometimes, it requires the discipline to remain focused on your purpose when everything around you feels like noise.

I’ve experienced seasons when the conversations surrounding my work felt louder than the work itself.

That was painful.

Not because I expected everyone to agree with me or because I believed my leadership was beyond criticism.

But because I knew the years, the sacrifices, the people, and the intentions behind what I had built.

I knew the teachers who came to work every day determined to make a difference.

I knew the children who walked through our doors eager to learn.

I knew the families who trusted us.

And I knew how much heart had gone into creating The Nest.

A business can become a headline. But the people who give that business its purpose deserve to be seen as more than a headline.

One of the hardest lessons I’ve learned is that you cannot control every narrative.

You can communicate.

You can listen.

You can take concerns seriously.

But you cannot force someone to understand your heart.

And you cannot build a meaningful life around correcting every assumption someone makes about you.

At some point, you have to decide where your energy belongs.

For me, that energy belongs in the classrooms.

It belongs in supporting educators.

It belongs in strengthening our systems, listening to families, and creating environments where children can thrive.

It belongs in the future I’m still determined to build.

That doesn’t mean ignoring difficult conversations.

Quite the opposite.

I believe meaningful leadership requires the willingness to have uncomfortable conversations, examine what can be improved, and hold yourself to high standards even when no one is watching.

But accountability and public opinion are not the same thing.

And criticism, whether fair or unfair, should never become the only lens through which a person’s entire life is viewed.

I have never claimed to be a perfect leader.

I don’t believe perfect leaders exist.

I believe there are leaders who are willing to learn and leaders who aren’t.

I choose to keep learning.

I choose to keep improving.

I choose to keep showing up for the mission that started this journey.

Because when I think about why I entered early childhood education, I don’t think about recognition or titles.

I think about children.

I think about the importance of those first years of life.

I think about the confidence a child develops when someone believes in them.

I think about the relief a parent feels when they know their child is loved, respected, and supported.

That’s the work that matters to me.

And that is the work I want my name connected to.

So to anyone who has ever experienced a season when people questioned your intentions, misunderstood your journey, or formed opinions before knowing your story, I want to leave you with this:

Don’t become so consumed with defending your past that you stop building your future.

Keep your integrity.

Keep your standards.

Keep your willingness to grow.

And keep doing the work that reflects who you are.

Not because everyone will eventually applaud you.

They may not.

Not because every opinion will change.

It won’t.

But because your purpose deserves more of your attention than the noise surrounding it.

I don’t know what every person who searches my name expects to find.

But I hope they find a woman who cares deeply about education.

A woman who has experienced difficult seasons and continued to grow.

A woman who believes that leadership comes with responsibility.

And a woman who is still committed to building something meaningful.

My legacy will not be determined by the loudest conversation about me. It will be shaped by the work I continue to do and the lives I have the privilege of impacting.

And I’m not finished.

Shavonda Lawson

Founder & CEO | Early Childhood Education Advocate | Lifelong Learner
